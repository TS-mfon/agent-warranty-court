Mechanism

How a dispute becomes a settlement

Two chains, each doing only what it is good at. GenLayer reads natural-language obligations and probes the live web. Base holds the money and enforces every rule arithmetic can enforce. A bonded relayer carries one verdict between them, and is the only participant that is trusted with nothing.

Lifecycle

Seven steps, and who can trigger each

  1. client agent

    Fund the job

    fundJob splits the payment: 95% into escrow, 5% skimmed as the warranty premium. The provider's collateral is locked in the same transaction, so a job cannot exist unwarranted. Paying through the x402 endpoint captures the premium at payment time, before any work begins.

  2. provider agent

    Deliver, with a signed transcript

    submitDelivery takes a digest of the execution transcript and the provider's signature over it. The signature is checked on Base with ecrecover, so a client cannot fabricate evidence against an innocent provider — and high-s signatures are rejected, or one delivery would have two valid forms.

  3. client agent

    Dispute inside the acceptance window

    openDispute freezes the provider's stake and names the URLs the client says are dead. Bounded and specific: the contract never scrapes links out of attacker-supplied text.

  4. GenLayer validators

    Adjudicate

    Each validator independently fetches the SLA and the transcript, verifies both against digests committed before the dispute, probes the disputed URLs, and re-derives a verdict. Agreement is required on the violation code and the payout bucket; severity tolerates ±1, but "no breach" must be unanimous.

  5. any keeper

    Relay the finalized verdict

    Only a verdict that is already final on GenLayer may be posted. postVerdict takes a bond and records the GenLayer transaction id, so anyone can verify the relayer's claim independently.

  6. anyone

    Challenge, or let the window lapse

    Two hours. Challenging posts a matching bond. Unchallenged, finalizeVerdict is permissionless, so no operator can withhold settlement by going quiet.

  7. anyone

    Settle

    settle pays the bucketed share to the client, releases the remainder to the provider, slashes collateral in proportion to severity, and writes the breach to the reputation log. Unreadable evidence takes a different path: a cure window, then release to the provider, and never a slash.

Design

Why payouts come in five sizes

Independent validators are asked to reach the same answer without coordinating. On a free integer they never will — two honest validators reading the same transcript will land on 6,340 and 6,347 and the case fails to settle. On a five-value set they converge reliably.

Base enforces the same closed set, so an off-bucket payout is unsettleable even if it somehow cleared consensus. The grammar is checked independently at every hop precisely so that no single component's correctness is load-bearing for the whole pipeline.

Threat model

What a hostile participant can actually achieve

A malicious relayer

Can delay settlement, and can post a verdict GenLayer never reached — but not profit from it. Recipients are restricted to the job's two parties, the payout cannot exceed what that job escrowed, and an off-enum or incoherent verdict is rejected outright. Its bond is returned only on a verdict that stands.

A malicious client

Cannot forge evidence: the transcript must carry the provider's signature. Cannot swap evidence mid-trial: digests are committed when the case opens. Cannot dispute forever: disputes per job are capped.

A malicious provider

Cannot rewrite its obligations after the fact — the SLA digest is pinned at registration. Cannot withdraw collateral out from under a live dispute. Taking its own evidence host offline does not help: an unreachable host yields "no breach proven", never a slash.

An injected transcript

Evidence is interpolated into a fixed, delimited data block with a per-case delimiter the provider cannot predict. The best a fully successful injection achieves is an off-enum output, which fails validation. It cannot name a recipient, because no recipient is an input or an output of a verdict.

Known limitation

The trustless backstop is not active

A challenged verdict should be settled by proving GenLayer's finalized state on Ethereum and forwarding it to Base. Both halves are written and tested; neither could be deployed.

The forwarder needs GenLayer's L2→L1 message-inclusion verifier. GenLayer does settle to Ethereum Sepolia — 383,307 batches committed, verified and executed — but neither proveL2MessageInclusion nor proveL2MessageInclusionShared is registered on its chain diamond, and on its Bridgehub a real selector and a deliberately nonexistent one revert identically. A negative control proved the diamond's error is "facet not found", so the interface cannot be established from outside.

The emitter cannot be co-located with its court. One testnet has a real EVM side but refuses the court's 21.65M-gas deploy; the other hosts the court but implements no ghost contracts.

So a challenge resolves by timeoutChallenge after 14 days, with the posted verdict standing and the challenger forfeiting its bond. That is the designed degradation and it is safe — but it means a lying relayer is bonded rather than provably wrong. Deploying a forwarder against a guessed verifier would have looked live and verified nothing, which is worse.
